The scope should name these editions (titles and numbers only):

**Editions and transition**: The 1.0 edition of this manual was written against ISO 9001:2015 and ISO 14001:2015. ISO 14001:2026 was published in April 2026 and ISO 9001:2026 on 16 September 2026; certification bodies expect a three-year transition, with ISO 14001:2015 certificates valid until about April 2029 and ISO 9001:2015 certificates until about September 2029 [to confirm: the final IAF transition dates with the certification body]. ISO/IEC 27001:2022 remains the current edition, together with its Amendment 1:2024 (climate action changes). Since Swedwise is not yet certified, it should seek initial certification directly against the 2026 editions of ISO 9001 and ISO 14001 rather than certify to 2015 and transition later; the clause references across the IMS documents (written against the 2015 editions) need to be checked against the 2026 editions before the certification audit [to confirm: the decision to certify against the 2026 editions].

**Proposal - not decided: ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (Artificial intelligence - Management system)**. Swedwise uses AI in its own operations and development (AI agents and AI-assisted development on its own platform, AI services in Microsoft Azure) and implements AI-enabled products for customers (for example OpenText Content Aviator and Salesforce AI features). Customers in public procurement may start asking how AI is governed, and the EU AI Act places obligations on deployers and providers. ISO/IEC 42001 follows the same harmonized structure as the three standards in scope, so it could be added to the IMS later at modest cost. Proposal: keep it out of the first certification scope, add an AI use inventory and AI risks to the integrated risk register now, and decide on ISO/IEC 42001 at the first management review after certification [to confirm: Management Team decision].

### 5.1 Process Approach

Swedwise manages the IMS as a network of interconnected processes. Each process has:
- **Inputs**: Resources, information, or materials entering the process
- **Activities**: Work performed to transform inputs
- **Outputs**: Results delivered by the process
- **Controls**: Policies, procedures, metrics governing the process
- **Ownership**: A process owner, named as a job, accountable for performance
- **Interactions**: Links to other processes (upstream inputs, downstream outputs)

### 5.2 IMS Process Model

**The master is the process register in Nexta Hub.** The register (`docs/management-system/processes.json` in the Swedwise.Nexta repository) holds the 26 processes, their groups, owners, steps, documents and hand-overs, and the hub's process map draws from it. The tables below are a reading of that register on 2026-10-29; where they differ, the register wins and this section is updated at the next revision.

The processes are organized into three groups. The 1.0 edition's "Monitoring and improvement processes" are folded into the management processes; customer feedback and complaints is a core process because it faces the customer. Owners are jobs; the IMS role the 1.0 edition named is given in brackets where it differs.

### 5.4 Process Ownership and Accountability

Each process has a designated **Process Owner** (a job) who:
- Ensures the process is documented and understood
- Monitors process performance against objectives
- Identifies and implements improvements
- Ensures resources are available
- Reports performance to management

**Process Ownership Matrix**: The owner column of the process register (section 5.2), reviewed annually.

### 5.5 Process Performance

Processes are measured using relevant KPIs. Performance is:
- Monitored regularly (monthly, quarterly, or real-time depending on process)
- Reported in management reviews
- Analyzed for trends and improvement opportunities
- Compared to objectives and targets

**Process Performance Dashboard**: Maintained by the Quality Lead (Head of PMO & Continuous Improvement), reviewed in management reviews.

### 5.6 Documented Information for Processes

Each critical process is supported by documented information:

| Documentation Level | Examples |
|-------------------|----------|
| **Policies** | High-level direction (e.g., Quality Policy, Information Security Policy) |
| **Procedures** | Step-by-step "what" and "who" (e.g., SW-IMS-PRO-003 Internal Audit Procedure) |
| **Work Instructions** | Detailed "how" (e.g., checklists, templates) |
| **Records** | Evidence of execution (e.g., audit reports, training records); for the core and finance processes, the records in Nexta Hub (deals, quotes, contracts, staffing, time, invoicing basis, support cases) |

Documentation is controlled according to SW-IMS-PRO-001 - Document Control Procedure.

## 6. Integration Across Management Systems

### 6.1 Unified Approach

Swedwise's IMS integrates quality, environmental, and information security management systems into a single coherent framework:

**Common Elements**:
- Single context and stakeholder analysis (this document and SW-IMS-REG-006)
- Integrated risk management (SW-IMS-PRO-002)
- Unified document control (SW-IMS-PRO-001)
- Combined internal audit program (SW-IMS-PRO-003)
- Integrated management review (SW-IMS-PRO-004)
- Shared corrective action process (SW-IMS-PRO-005)

**Discipline-Specific Elements**:
- **Quality (QMS)**: Customer satisfaction, project delivery, service performance
- **Environmental (EMS)**: Environmental aspects (travel, energy, waste), objectives
- **Information Security (ISMS)**: Risk assessment, controls, incident management

### 6.2 Alignment with Business Strategy

The IMS is not a separate "compliance system" but is integrated with business strategy:
- IMS objectives align with strategic business objectives
- IMS processes are the business processes (not parallel bureaucracy)
- IMS supports business goals (customer satisfaction, growth, risk management)
- IMS enables market access (public procurement, customer requirements)
